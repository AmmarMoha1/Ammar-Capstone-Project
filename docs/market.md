# Wajha — Market Validation

The capstone guide asks us to **separate evidence from assumptions**. Everything under "Assumptions" is unproven until the survey and interview results below are filled in. Please don't present an assumption as a finding.

## Problem statement

Groups of friends in Saudi Arabia planning an international leisure trip struggle to agree on a destination. Each person has a different budget, maximum flight time, weather preference and set of interests, and the discussion usually happens in a long, unstructured group chat.

## Assumptions (to be tested)

| # | Assumption | How we test it |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Groups of friends often take a long time, or fail, to agree on a destination | Survey Q3–Q5, interviews |
| A2 | Budget and flight duration are the most common reasons a destination is rejected | Survey Q6 |
| A3 | People don't always share their real budget openly in the group chat | Survey Q7, interviews |
| A4 | Organizers would use a tool that collects everyone's constraints and ranks destinations | Survey Q8–Q9, demo reactions |
| A5 | Users would click through to book flights or hotels from a decided trip (affiliate revenue) | Survey Q10 |

## Evidence

### Survey (target: 10–15 people aged 18–35 who travelled abroad with friends)

Questions:
1. Age range, and the city you usually travel from
2. How many international trips with friends have you taken in the last 3 years?
3. How was the destination decided last time? (one person decided / group chat / poll / other)
4. Roughly how long did it take to agree? (same day / a few days / a week or more / never agreed)
5. Did anyone drop out or end up unhappy with the choice? Why?
6. What usually rules a destination out? (budget / flight length / no direct flight / weather / activities / visa / other)
7. Would you be comfortable sharing your exact budget in the group chat? (yes / prefer not / no)
8. Would you use an app where everyone enters their preferences privately and it ranks destinations for the group? (1–5)
9. What would make you trust its recommendation?
10. After deciding, would you book flights or hotels through links in the same app? (yes / maybe / no)

## Existing tools and manual processes

This is a general comparison of what each tool is designed for. Check current features before presenting.

| Option | What it does well | Why it doesn't solve this problem |
|---|---|---|
| Group chat discussion (e.g. WhatsApp) | Everyone is already there; free | Unstructured; budgets stay unspoken; no way to weigh everyone's constraints |
| Group chat polls | Quick vote between a few options | Someone must choose the options first; a majority vote can ignore one member's hard limits |
| Flight "explore anywhere" searches (e.g. Skyscanner) | Find cheap destinations from one origin | Designed around one traveller's budget, not several people's constraints and interests |
| Itinerary planners (e.g. Wanderlog) | Plan a trip once the destination is known | Start after the decision Wajha helps make |
| Expense-splitting apps (e.g. Splitwise) | Share costs during and after a trip | Solve a later stage of the trip |

**Why choose Wajha:** each member's constraints are collected privately, destinations that break anyone's limit are excluded, and the remaining options are ranked with a fairness rule. This is the step the existing tools leave to a group chat.

## Adoption and revenue model (assumptions)

- **Adoption:** free for groups. One organizer creates a trip and shares a code in the existing group chat; friends join in under a minute. Each trip brings in new users.
- **Revenue:** affiliate commissions on flight, hotel and activity bookings made after a destination is decided (tests assumption A5). Possibly later, a premium tier for larger groups or detailed itinerary planning.
- **Scope today:** the capstone delivers the decision step only; booking and payments are future work.
